How to use it

Five steps, about a minute the first time.

Steps

  1. Tell it where your class is

    On Context, pick your course and tick the topics you've covered. Problems you haven't reached are marked "not covered yet".

  2. Pick a problem

    On Check work, choose a practice problem or "My own problem". Got a photo of your page? Open "Upload a page" to keep it beside you while you type.

  3. Write one step per line

    Press Enter for a new line. Copy your own steps, mistakes and all. That's the point.

  4. Press Check my work

    A line sweeps down your page. Lines that follow get a tick. The first one that doesn't gets a note in the margin: what went wrong, why, and what to try next.

  5. Say how it landed

    "I get it now" logs it on your Profile. "Still stuck" gives a smaller hint. Press it again on a practice problem to unlock the worked solution.

How the checking works

It doesn't guess and it doesn't use AI. It plugs numbers into your line and the line before it, using the math.js library. If two expressions agree at a dozen random values, the step holds. Two equations hold if they keep the same solution.

So it's precise about whether a step follows, but not about why. The "why" on practice problems is written by hand. On your own problems it describes what it measured, for example "line 3 is off by 12 from the line before it".

It can't read words, graphs or proofs. It can't read an uploaded page by itself either: that would need an AI model running on a server, which this laptop version doesn't have.
